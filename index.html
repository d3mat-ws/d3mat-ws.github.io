About the Workshop

This workshop introduces beginners to the fundamentals of Density Functional Theory (DFT), one of the most widely used computational methods for studying the electronic, structural, and optical properties of materials. Through lectures, tutorials, and hands-on sessions, participants will explore the basic principles of quantum mechanics underlying DFT, learn how to set up and perform simulations using modern DFT software, and gain practical experience in analyzing computational results. The workshop aims to build a strong conceptual foundation, promote the effective use of first-principles simulations in research, and encourage participants to apply DFT techniques to problems in materials science, physics, chemistry, and engineering.

Talk title: Modelling site-disordered solids beyond thermodynamics: DFT, statistical mechanics and machine learning
Abstract

This talk will present a perspective on the modelling of site disorder in crystalline solids, with emphasis on approaches that go beyond simple energetic descriptions. I will first outline the main methodological landscape for treating site disorder, from mean-field and cluster-expansion strategies to explicit configurational and ensemble-based approaches. I will then focus on symmetry-adapted ensemble models [1], which make it possible to combine statistical mechanics with atomistic calculations such as DFT in order to predict not only configurational thermodynamics, but also properties that are often difficult to capture with conventional cluster expansions, including cell parameters, effective physical properties and average spectra (such as NMR [2, 3]). Examples will be used to illustrate how ensemble models can provide a realistic description of disordered materials when local geometry, long-range interactions, or property averaging are essential. Finally, I will discuss recent work on generative AI for crystal structures through the CrystaLLM project [4], and a possible route to address one of its current limitations: the treatment of site disorder. I will introduce the idea of a crystal virtualiser that maps explicit ordered supercells back to virtual disordered representations with fractional occupancies, enabling a posteriori correction of AI-generated crystal structures.

Talk title: Fundamentals of Density Functional Theory
Abstract

Density Functional Theory (DFT) is a powerful first-principles approach for understanding the electronic structure and properties of materials at the atomic scale. This talk will introduce the fundamental concepts of DFT, including the many-body problem, Born–Oppenheimer approximation, Hohenberg–Kohn theorems, Kohn–Sham equations, exchange–correlation functionals, and self-consistent field calculations. The talk will also briefly discuss practical aspects of DFT calculations and their applications in predicting structural, electronic, and optical properties of materials.

Hands-on Session — Modelling of Materials
Abstract

This hands-on session introduces participants to the computer-aided modelling of materials and crystal structures using VESTA. The session will focus on the practical construction and visualization of different structural models that are commonly used in computational materials research.

Participants will learn how to generate and visualize supercells from primitive or conventional crystal structures, with emphasis on understanding periodicity and selecting appropriate cell dimensions for computational studies. The session will further demonstrate the modelling of surfaces and slab structures, including the selection of crystallographic orientations and creation of surface models for studying surface-related properties.

The session will also introduce nanoparticle modelling, providing participants with an understanding of how bulk crystal structures can be transformed into finite-sized structures. Through guided exercises in VESTA, participants will gain practical experience in building, modifying, visualizing, and exporting material structures suitable for subsequent computational calculations using tools such as Quantum ESPRESSO.

Hands-on Session — Quantum ESPRESSO & Input file preparartion
Abstract

This hands-on session provides participants with a practical introduction to Quantum ESPRESSO and the preparation of input files for first-principles calculations. The session will guide participants through the essential components of a Quantum ESPRESSO input file, including crystal structure, atomic species, lattice parameters, atomic positions, pseudopotentials, k-point sampling, energy cutoffs, and calculation parameters.

Participants will learn how to construct and modify input files for different computational tasks and understand the role of each parameter in determining the accuracy and efficiency of the calculations. The session will also demonstrate how to organize the input and output files and execute calculations using Quantum ESPRESSO.

Through guided hands-on exercises, participants will develop the skills required to prepare, validate, and run Quantum ESPRESSO calculations, establishing a foundation for subsequent calculations such as structure optimization, band structure, and density of states analysis.

Hands-on Session — Quantum ESPRESSO calculations
Abstract

This hands-on session introduces participants to first-principles electronic-structure calculations using Quantum ESPRESSO, with Burai serving as a user-friendly graphical interface for setting up and managing the calculations. Participants will learn the practical workflow for investigating the structural and electronic properties of materials, starting from the preparation of crystal structures and calculation parameters.

The session will provide guided experience in structure optimization, where the atomic positions and lattice parameters are relaxed to obtain a stable configuration. The optimized structure will then be used to perform electronic band structure calculations to understand the dispersion of electronic states across high-symmetry points in the Brillouin zone. Participants will also carry out density of states (DOS) calculations to examine the distribution of electronic states and gain insights into the electronic characteristics of the material.

By the end of the session, participants will have practical experience with the complete workflow—from structure preparation and optimization to band structure and DOS analysis—and will be able to interpret the resulting computational data using Burai and Quantum ESPRESSO

Hands-on Session — Tutorial On How To Install Quantum ESPRESSO
Abstract

This hands-on session provides participants with a practical guide to installing and setting up essential software tools for computational materials modelling. The session will focus on the installation and basic configuration of Quantum ESPRESSO (QE) on Linux platforms, enabling participants to establish a working environment for first-principles calculations.

Participants will be guided through the installation of BURAI, a graphical user interface (GUI) for Quantum ESPRESSO, to simplify the preparation and management of QE calculations. The session will also cover the installation and setup of VESTA for crystal structure visualization and modelling, along with XCrySDen for interactive visualization of crystal structures, surfaces, and computational results.

Through step-by-step demonstrations and guided installation, participants will gain the practical skills needed to set up a complete computational materials modelling environment, preparing them for the subsequent hands-on sessions involving structure modelling, input-file preparation, and Quantum ESPRESSO calculations.
